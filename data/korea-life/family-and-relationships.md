# Korea in numbers: relationships, marriage, children and family life

Scope: official Korean statistics on adult partnership, marriage, divorce, childbearing, parental leave and child benefits, work–family balance and later family life, for an adult-life extension of the Suneung Simulator. It extends `data/korea-stats.md` section 3 (divorce totals 2024–25, single-parent and care-system rows) and section 4 (daycare and kindergarten); rows already there are not repeated except where a newer release replaces them (2025 final births and divorces).

Sources read: attachments on mods.go.kr (국가데이터처) boards were downloaded and read in full unless a row says otherwise: `2025년 혼인·이혼 통계` (hwpx, bid 204, list 444103), `2025년 출생 통계` (hwpx, bid 204, list 446625), `2026년 8월 인구동향` (pdf, bid 204, list 447664), `지난 30년간 우리나라의 혼인·출생 변화` (pdf, bid 204, list 438375), `2025년 다문화 인구동태 통계` (pdf, bid 204, list 447832), `2024년 사회조사 결과(가족 …)` (pdf, bid 219, list 433638), `2024년 생활시간조사 결과` (hwpx, bid 220, list 437764), `생활시간조사로 본 청년의 혼인 및 자녀 유무별 시간사용 변화` (pdf, bid 220, list 447502), `2024년 육아휴직통계 결과(잠정)` (hwpx, bid 11814, list 442470), `2024년 신혼부부통계 결과` (hwpx, bid 11815, list 442387), `2025년 상반기 지역별고용조사 기혼여성의 고용 현황` (pdf, bid 211, list 439372), `2025년 하반기 맞벌이 가구 및 1인 가구 취업 현황` (pdf, bid 211, list 445516), `2026 고령자 통계` (pdf, bid 10820, list 447294). Ministry pages (mohw.go.kr, moel.go.kr, kca.go.kr) were read as page text; the MAFRA pet survey was read from its press-release pdf on korea.kr.

Currency: 만원 = ₩10,000, 억 = ₩100,000,000. Rates "per 1,000" are per 1,000 people of that sex and age unless stated.

## Game ideas

- **Marriage hazard by age:** use the 2025 age-specific marriage rates as a yearly chance for a single player (women 25–29: ~4.4%, 30–34: ~5.8%, 35–39: ~2.1%; men 30–34: ~5.4%), then raise it for a player in a relationship. Mean first marriage at 33.9 (men) / 31.6 (women) and only 39% of women 30–34 married are good checks for a simulated cohort.
- **Why you don't marry:** the 사회조사 reasons map to blockers: money (31%) → savings or housing threshold, job insecurity (13%) → contract work, childcare burden (15%) → a "fear" stat. Never-married women are far less keen (26% "should marry") than men (42%), so a partner's willingness can depend on sex and outlook.
- **Wedding costs:** a ₩21M baseline wedding with a Gangnam premium (venue ₩31M vs Busan ₩8M), a peak-season markup, and 77% of people thinking it excessive invite a "small wedding" option.
- **First child after marriage:** newlywed curve: 22% have a child after year 1, 57% by year 3, 73% by year 5; marital fertility 20%/year at 25–34, 7.6% at 35–39, 1% at 40–44. Home ownership (+9 points) and a non-working wife (+8) raise the chance; dual earners lower it.
- **Infertility route:** after 35 the natural chance falls; offer state-funded IVF (25 cycles, 30% co-pay, ~37% success, lower after 40). One birth in five in 2025 came through the programme.
- **Birth order and age:** first births at 33.2, second at 34.7, third at 35.8; only 6.4% of births are third+, so each extra child should feel rare and costly. Seoul's TFR (0.63) vs Jeonnam (1.09) can scale regional fertility.
- **Parental leave choice:** mothers who are eligible employees take leave 72% of the time, fathers 10% (16% within a year); use firm size and sector (public 16%, big firm 12.5%) for the father's chance. Pay the 2025 caps (₩2.5M → 2.0M → 1.6M) and the 6+6 bonus if both parents take it.
- **Cash for babies:** ₩2–3M at birth, ₩1M/month at age 0, ₩0.5M at age 1, ₩100k/month child allowance until 9 (13 by 2030).
- **Career break:** for a mother with a child under 7, a ~32% chance of being out of work for family reasons, and 42% of breaks last 10+ years; it should lower later pay.
- **Housework clock:** a married mother of young children loses ~3.5 hours a day to housework and care versus being single; a dual-earner wife still does ~1h43m more than her husband. Couples whose split matches the 69% "should be equal" ideal could earn a satisfaction bonus.
- **Divorce hazard:** ~3.5 per 1,000 married people a year, peaking in the 40s; one divorce in three ends a marriage of 20+ years, so grey divorce is a real late-game event. Remarriage ages ~52 / 47.
- **Old age:** by the late game 38% of older households are one person; family care dominates but long-term-care insurance is rising; 24% of older people plan to spend their savings rather than leave them, and only 6.5% favour the eldest son.
- **Pets:** about 3 in 10 households keep a pet (mostly dogs), at ~₩121k a month.

- **Dating and relationships:** no official figure found for the share of people in their 20s–30s currently dating or in a relationship. KIHASA's 2024 가족과 출산 조사 likely has it, but its report download (kihasa.re.kr file API) returned "page not found"; the 저출산고령사회위원회 attitude surveys (2024–26) were seen only in news.
- **Cohabitation prevalence:** only attitudes (67.4% accept) are official; no count of cohabiting couples found (register data cannot see them; the 2025 census 20% sample results are due end-November 2026).
- **How couples meet:** no official survey found.
- **Marriage by income or education:** no official table found of married shares of men by income decile (studies cite KOSIS microdata or KLI papers, not a release); newlywed income is the closest official proxy.
- **Childlessness:** only the newlywed figure (48.8% at 0–5 years married) is official; lifetime childlessness by birth cohort was not found.
- **Cost of raising a child:** no current official estimate; the often-quoted KIHASA figure (around ₩300M to age 22, 2012 survey) was not verified.
- **Grandparents caring for grandchildren:** no current official share found (the 보육실태조사 would have it; not read).
- **Infertility treatment counts:** HIRA patient counts for 2024–25 were not read; only births through the support programme are given.
- **Housework by sex for all adults:** the 2024 time-use release gives couples, employed people and the elderly, but the all-adult male/female totals were not read from the tables.
