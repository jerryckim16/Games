# Korea in numbers: society, daily life and leisure for adults

Scope: official Korean statistics on how adults spend their time, their leisure and consumption, wellbeing and attitudes, civic life, military service, crime and safety, digital life, and migration, for an adult-life extension of the Suneung Simulator. It extends `data/korea-stats.md` sections 9 (students' lives), 13–15 (after school, jobs, military service lengths and the job hunt) and does not repeat their rows.

Sources read: these attachments were downloaded and read in full: `2024년 생활시간조사 결과.pdf` (mods.go.kr bid 220, list 437764), `2025년 사회조사 결과 보도자료.pdf` and `(통계표).xlsx` (bid 219, list 439196), `2024년 사회조사 결과 통계표.xlsx` (bid 219, list 433638), `국민 삶의 질 2025 보도자료_최종.pdf` and `보고서_펼침.pdf` (bid 246, list 443849), MCST press-release PDFs for the 2025 reading survey (pSeq 22278) and the 2025 leisure/culture surveys (pSeq 22169), NEC `제21대 대통령선거 투표율 분석 결과` PDF, and the MSIT hwpx releases for the 2025 internet-use survey and the 2025 digital-divide / smartphone-overdependence survey (attached to korea.kr briefings). e-나라지표 (index.go.kr) rows were read from the indicator's own statistics table (the `showStblGams3.do` frame), which states the source yearbook. Rows tagged **unverified** come from news or secondary sites, mostly read from search-result text rather than the article page, because the primary host (immigration.go.kr, moj.go.kr, police.go.kr, mma.go.kr, kcti.re.kr, spo.go.kr) refused connections from this session. See `data/README.md` for the status tags.

Currency: 만원 = ₩10,000, 억 = ₩100,000,000, 조 = ₩1,000,000,000,000. Time is h:mm per day unless noted. 사회조사 covers people 13+ (19+ where noted); 생활시간조사 covers people 10+.

## Game ideas

- **Weekly time budget for adults.** Give an employed adult about 35 h of paid work (men 38, women 32), 10–11 h of commuting and other travel, and 56 h of sleep per week, then let the player spend the rest. Women in dual-earner couples with a child spend 3:32 a day on housework and care against husbands' 1:24; let the player choose how to split it, with marriage satisfaction reacting (only 37% of working wives are satisfied with the split).
- **Age shapes free time.** The 30s have the least leisure (3:59 a day) and the 60+ the most (6:36). Games fall from 1:20 a day in the teens to 0:13 in the 40s; sport rises with age. A "no time" event chain fits ages 30–49.
- **Life satisfaction drivers.** Base 6.4/10, with income shifting it about ±0.4 (5.8 under ₩1M a month, 6.5 above ₩3M), loneliness doubling among the dissatisfied, and isolation rising with age (24% in the 20s, 39% at 60+). Having someone to borrow money from (51%) and someone to talk to (79%) can be hidden stats that buffer bad events.
- **Military service choice at 19–20.** About 87% of examined men are sent to active duty, 7.6% to supplementary service (social-service agent), 2–3% unfit or exempt, 3% re-examined. Pay from ₩750k (private) to ₩1.5M (sergeant) plus up to ₩550k a month matched savings, which makes a discharge lump sum of several million won a fair reward.
- **Travel and reading as lifestyle traits.** 70% travel domestically (3.9 trips a year), 31.5% abroad (peaking at 43% in the 30s). Adult reading collapses with age (75% in the 20s, 14% at 60+) and with low income, so a "reader" trait could decay unless maintained.
- **Civic events.** Voting turnout by age (20s 52–75%, 60s–70s 82–88%) can drive a "did you vote?" prompt with different default odds by age. Volunteering is mostly a teen requirement (67%) and drops to 6% in the 30s.
- **Risk events.** Voice phishing (about 21,000 cases and ₩8,500억 in 2024, ₩53.8M per case in 2025) is a plausible random financial shock for older characters. Women feel unsafe at night three times as often as men (44.9% vs 15.8%).
- **Class mobility mood.** Only 29% think effort can raise their position, and the 40s are the most pessimistic. This can be a flavour stat that moves with the player's income fifth.
- **Digital life.** AI use (44.5% generative) and smartphone overdependence (22.7%; 43% among teens) can serve as era markers in the late-game timeline.
