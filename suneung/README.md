# Suneung Simulator (수능 시뮬레이터)

A life-sim about the Korean college entrance exam, 대학수학능력시험 (수능). You're born at random into one of Korea's five household-income fifths, with a temperament your parents notice in your first weeks and a head start from what you grab at your first-birthday 돌잡이. You play the baby and kindergarten years, start elementary school at 6 and grow up through middle school and high school, deciding each turn how to spend your time: which subjects to study, whether your family pays for cram school, when to read, rest, play or see friends. life happens along the way. In high school every midterm and final counts toward your school grades (내신), and what you do goes on your school record (학생부). At the end of High 1 you choose humanities or sciences. In High 3 you play month by month: in September you send up to six early applications (수시) on your grades, your record or an essay exam, and in November you sit the real 수능. Early offers come in December; without one, you apply in regular admission (정시) with your score. Then take an offer or retake the exam the next year (재수, 삼수, N수) as many times as you like.

Open `index.html` in a browser. There's no build step and no dependencies. Progress autosaves in the browser. It's also published as a single-file claude.ai artifact: https://claude.ai/artifact/H8zzP1PqsHVdvEQEARTuFE

**Play it in English or Korean.** The switch in the top corner changes every word on the page, including the life log of a game in progress, and the choice is remembered. The first visit follows the browser's language.

When you enroll (or put the pencil down), you can live the next ten years: a major, university life, military service for men, the job hunt, and where you are at 30, with your pay ranked against the family you were born into. Achievements and your past lives carry over from one life to the next, and every life ends with a share card.

## How to play

School years are counted the Korean way, with each school numbering its own years: Elementary 1–6 (초1–6), Middle 1–3 (중1–3) and High 1–3 (고1–3). High 3 (고3) is the 수능 year.

1. **Be born.** Choose boy or girl and type a name. Your family and your talents are dealt at random; you can't pick or re-roll them.
   - The birth screen shows where you're born, with a pin on a map of the peninsula, your family's household income, which fifth of Korean households it's in, and what your parents can spend on your education each month.
   - The talent bars show aptitude, which is how fast you learn each subject (×1.00 is average), with stars. Every child is quicker than average at something: the best subject's aptitude is at least ×1.10. Ability itself starts near 0 for every baby, since it's on the 수능 scale. The stars stay on screen all game (see Aptitude below).
   - Then your first weeks reveal your **temperament**, and at your first birthday the **돌잡이** shows what you grab. Both give you a trait: a permanent bonus (see Traits below).
   - A fortune-teller's reading (사주) tells you how lucky a life you've been dealt. Luck runs through everything from here on (see Luck below).
   - Ages 0–5 are played a year at a time, with play instead of study, and a kindergarten choice at 3.
2. **Pick a school** at 3 (kindergarten) and at the start of elementary, middle and high school. The local public school is always open; private and special schools have fees, lotteries or entry requirements (see Schools below).
3. **Grow up.** Each turn you assign blocks of free time. You can also **move house** to another region (once a year) from the **Life choices ⋯** menu, or by tapping the map (on a phone, the 📍 place chip):

   | Stage | One turn is | Blocks |
   |---|---|---|
   | Early childhood (age 0–5) | a year | 3 |
   | Elementary school (초1–6, Elementary 1–6, age 6–11) | a school year | 4 |
   | Middle school (중1–3, Middle 1–3) | a semester | 4 |
   | High school (고1–2, High 1–2) | a season: spring term, summer break, fall term, winter break | 4, or 5 in the breaks |
   | Final year (고3, High 3) | a month (March to November) | 4, 6 in July–August, 2 in 수능 month |
   | Retake years | a month | 6 (반수: 2 until June) |

   Pick a study method too; it applies to every study block that turn.

   From Middle 1 you can also **leave school** (자퇴) from the Life choices menu. Out of school you get two more blocks a turn in Middle 1 to High 2 but lose the school lessons, which otherwise raise every subject a little each turn on their own. See Leaving school below.
4. **Handle events.** Most turns bring one: a sports day, a math olympiad, the smartphone debate, a move to Daechi-dong, class elections, 중2, a PC방 invitation, a crush, a slump, a star lecturer's new course. Some only need an OK; others ask you to choose. Some lives also run into bullying (학교폭력), which hangs over every turn until it's dealt with.
5. **Watch your standing.**
   - The elementary-school teacher writes a report-card comment (통지표) each year: how you're doing, what your free time went to (books, friends, games, sport), and your strongest subject. A worn-out teacher writes one line; a child who is being bullied just "seems quieter lately".
   - Middle school shows your class rank each semester.
   - High 1 and 2 sit mock exams (학력평가) in June and November.
   - From High 1 to the first semester of High 3, each semester has a midterm and a final (중간·기말고사) graded against your year at your school: a report card after each (see School grades below).
6. **Choose a track** at the end of High 1: humanities (확률과 통계 + two social-studies subjects) or sciences (미적분 + two science subjects; the only track that can apply to medical school). While humanities is selected, a red warning says it closes medical school for good and the button reads "Confirm humanities". Your social-studies ability carries into the humanities subjects, your science ability into the science ones.
7. **Sit the exams.**
   - High 3 has mock exams in March, June and September, with full score reports.
   - In November you make two last choices: how you spend the night before, and what's in your 수능 lunch box. The day itself may deal you some luck, good or bad. Then you sit the 수능 period by period.
   - The score report comes in December.
8. **Early admission (수시).** In September of High 3, after the September mock, send up to six applications by school grades (학생부교과), school record review (학생부종합) or essay exam (논술), each with its chance and any minimum 수능 grades. Record reviews with an interview announce a first round in October and interview after the 수능; final results, after the minimum-grade check, come in December. Any early offer rules out regular admission that year (수시 납치).
9. **Regular admission (정시) and the decision.** Without an early offer, apply to up to three universities (정시). The real cuts only show when the results come out, and a rejection can still turn into a February waitlist call. Then, with early or regular offers:
   - enroll;
   - enroll but secretly retake (반수);
   - retake at a 재수종합학원 or by self-study (독학재수);
   - or stop.

9. **Live the next ten years** (optional, from the ending screen): choose a major, how you spend university, when and where you serve if you're a man, and what to aim for in the job hunt. A few years later you see yourself at 30. See After the exam below.

The **Hall of fame** keeps your 10 best lives on this device, ranked by 국수탐 standard-score total. **Achievements** and **Past lives** sit under it on the new-life screen (and behind buttons beside the exam history during a life), and **Live today's life** starts the life everyone gets on today's date.

## The interface

Built for a first-time player on a phone as much as a desktop (checked at 390px and 1200px, light and dark, in both languages).

- **The turn bar.** The bottom of the plan sticks to the screen while you plan: the turn's blocks as slots (tap one to take it back), an "N of M blocks left" pill (read out to screen readers as it changes) and the **Next** button. On a desktop it sticks to the bottom of the plan panel. A double click on Next only plays one turn.
- **Activity cards.** Two compact columns: icon, name, aptitude stars, what the activity does as small coloured chips (green helps, red costs, amber is money), and a − / + stepper with 44px buttons. With every block used, + greys out and a one-time hint says how to free a block.
- **"This turn".** After each Next, a card of up to four chips sums up what the turn did: the biggest subject gain, the biggest change in stress or health, the money spent (or earned) and the class rank in middle school. It's read from the turn's log entry, so it never changes the game.
- **First-turn coach marks.** On a new player's first plan screen, three short pointers (the blocks and cards; lessons, ability bars and aptitude stars; Next), each with a highlight ring, a bubble and Skip. Shown once per browser (`suneung.coach.v1`).
- **Phone order.** On a phone the subtitle goes once a life starts, the map folds into a "📍 place · Move ›" chip, the basics become a two-row strip with the family as chips ([class meter] [who raises you] [Mom: job] [Dad: job]), and the plan comes before **Your stats** (ability, the school record, condition and the chart), which folds away.
- **Life choices ⋯.** Moving house, leaving school (in red) and starting a new life sit in a menu beside the plan's title, away from Next.
- **The map.** A legend (home, can move, closed) under the map, a hint that says why when nothing is open, and place names that stay at least 11px on screen: a small map only names home and the place picked, and Seoul's six districts are named only when picked (every dot still has its name on hover and for screen readers).
- **Dialogs** are real dialogs: the page behind them is inert, Tab stays inside, focus returns where it was when they close, and Escape cancels the optional ones (Move, Leave school, New life, Achievements, Past lives, Live again). Each dialog and cutscene has its own language switch, and switching keeps what you'd picked (application ticks, the track and its subjects, a school, the place on the move map). Buttons sit in a sticky footer, and irreversible choices (Leave school) are drawn in red with the safe answer as the primary button.
- **Applications.** Regular and early admission list schools by your chance, fold the long shots away (10% or less for 정시), pin your picks as chips at the top (tap one to take it back) and keep Apply in the sticky footer.
- **Korean typography:** words aren't broken mid-word (`keep-all`), with a little more line height, and each screen keeps one register (합니다체 for the interface, 한다체 for the story).
- **Accessible colour:** light mode's grade chips, chance pills, gold stars and warning colours are darkened to pass WCAG AA; dark mode keeps its own brighter set. The ability chart is labelled for screen readers with where each subject stands.

## Features

- **After the exam: the next ten years.** From the ending screen, an epilogue of a few screens with real choices, driven by real figures (see After the exam below):
  - **A major** from your track (humanities, social sciences, education and arts on humanities; engineering, natural sciences, nursing and health, education and arts on sciences; medicine for 의대), shown with its real employment rate.
  - **University years:** the library, a balance, an exchange semester (₩5M from your savings if your family can't pay), a year off for internships, or campus life. They set your GPA (out of 4.5) and your experience.
  - **Military service for men:** when (after year 1, year 2 or graduating) and where, with 2026's real lengths: army and marines 18 months, navy 20, air force 21; social service 21 if your health has been poor; alternative service 36; a military doctor after residency.
  - **The job hunt (취준):** a big company, a big company's production line (vocational high-school graduates), a public enterprise (blind hiring), the civil service exam (28.6 applicants a place in 2026), the teacher appointment exam, law school and the bar (about half pass each sitting), a hospital, a startup, graduate school, a small firm, a trade, a service job or your own shop; for doctors, a popular or an essential specialty or general practice. Chances depend on your university, major, GPA, experience, the ability you finished school with and a little on your family; a failed year lets you try again, take a small-firm job or work part-time.
  - **At 30:** your job, your pay, your rank among Korean earners next to the family you were born into (↑ or ↓, the honest heart of it), and where you live: Seoul for big firms and law, the 혁신도시 for public enterprises, home for teachers and civil servants; with your parents, a studio flat, a jeonse apartment, or one you own with your parents' help.
  - Everything rolls from its own seeded streams, so nothing before the ending moves, and an old save that already ended can play it too.
- **Achievements:** 44 of them, from a perfect paper, SNU, 의대 and a fifth try to 개천에서 용 났다 (bottom fifth to SKY or medicine), leaving school and still reaching Seoul, a children's-home child reaching university, a retake-academy scholarship, surviving bullying, every High 3 month at a cram school, and at 30 a big company, the civil service, the top fifth, or bottom to top. They stay unlocked across lives on this device; locked ones show a hint. A toast announces each new one.
- **Past lives:** the last 50 lives on this device with name, birth year, region, family, parents' jobs, outcome (and job at 30), best total, tries and achievements. **Live again** replays any of them from the same seed: the same family, talents and luck.
- **Today's life:** a seed from the date, so everyone who starts it today is born into the same life. Compare with friends.
- **A share card** at the end, drawn in SVG: your name, birth year and place, family, where you ended up, your best 수능 grades, tries and a five-step ladder from the family you were born into to your fifth at 30. Download it as a PNG (or press and hold the picture where downloads are blocked).
- **A whole childhood, from birth.** Ability is on the 수능 scale from the start (50 is the average test taker), so a baby begins far below it and climbs. Ability never decays: a subject you don't study stops growing but keeps what you learned. Early habits decide how far you climb.
- **Who raises you.** Most children are born to two parents; about 6% are born to a single mother, 2% to a single father, 0.7% are raised by grandparents (조손가정; grandparent households are under 1% of households) and 0.3% grow up in a children's home (보육원, see below). Each is rolled at birth from its own seeded stream and named on the birth screen.
  - **Divorce:** about one child in eight sees their parents divorce before High 3 (75% stay with their mother). The household drops an income fifth, with a stress and motivation hit and a lost block.
  - **Losing a parent:** about 3% of children lose a parent. With one left you live with them; if it was your only parent, your grandparents take you in (7 times in 10) or you go into a children's home. Some insurance money, a long grief (stress +25, motivation −12) and two lost blocks.
  - **Everyday effects:** with one parent the education budget is ×0.6 (single-parent household income is 60.3% of the average; grandparents ×0.5), stress settles 4 higher (3 with grandparents) and motivation 1 lower (3 with grandparents). The household's income fifth is still dealt as usual.
  - **The words follow:** with one parent or grandparents, every "your parents" in the game becomes "your mom", "your dad" or "your grandparents" (verbs and Korean particles adjusted), a missing parent is replaced by the one who's there, and cutscenes draw only the parents in your life (a grandmother when grandparents raise you).
  - The Family strip shows the setup next to the income class.
- **Parents' jobs.** Each parent you live with has a job, rolled at birth from its own seeded stream: company executive, doctor, professional (engineer, designer, instructor), software developer, teacher, nurse, civil servant, office worker, service worker, small-shop owner, salesperson, farmer, skilled tradesperson, driver, factory worker, cleaner or laborer, or staying at home.
  - **Real shares:** mothers follow the occupations of working married mothers with a child under 18, fathers the occupations of men aged 30–49 (2025 surveys), split into named jobs by official head counts (doctors, teachers, nurses, software experts, government clerks, drivers, shop and restaurant owners). About 58% of mothers work while you're under 7, rising to about 70% by your teens as mothers go back to work, and about 94% of fathers. See `data/korea-stats.md` (Jobs).
  - **Jobs fit the family:** the income fifth is dealt first, and jobs are drawn from a table fitted so that better-paid jobs (by each group's real wage bands) lean to richer fifths while every job keeps its national share. Farmers are far more common in rural places.
  - **Jobs change:** a layoff hits the better-paid parent more often and sends them into a small fried-chicken shop with the severance pay, driving, sales, service or labor; a shop "goes under" rather than laying anyone off; a parent left alone at home by a divorce or a death goes to work; a mother at home often goes back to work once school starts. The log records each change.
  - **Small effects:** a teacher at home makes reading count more and lifts motivation; a doctor parent lifts motivation and stress; civil servants and teachers are rarely laid off (×0.2) and promoted a little less; shop owners rise and fall more often (layoff ×1.6, promotion ×1.3); shift and manual jobs add a point of resting stress; a parent at home takes 2 off it.
  - **Events:** helping at the family shop on weekends (money, a lost block), a teacher parent checking every page of homework (Korean and English up), the medical-school talk with a doctor parent, night shifts, harvest weekends on the farm and a school play missed for a late meeting. Promotions and layoffs name the parent and the job.
  - **On screen:** the birth screen says what your parents do and draws them in work clothes (white coat, scrubs, apron, suit, hard hat, straw hat, driver's cap, hi-vis vest); the Family strip lists the jobs of the parents you live with.
  - Grandparents and children's homes have no parents' jobs; old saves have none and play as before.
- **Growing up in a children's home (보육원, 아동양육시설).** About 2,300–4,100 children a year come into state care in Korea, 63% of them into a home, so roughly 0.6% of a cohort spends time in one; in the game 0.3% start life there, and a child whose only parent dies goes there 3 times in 10.
  - **Money:** the home pays for school and gives about ₩150,000 a month for your education (an estimate; below the lowest fifth's ₩205,000), with nothing from income or fortune: promotions, layoffs, inheritances, scams, parents' illness and divorce never happen, and nobody pays for a retake academy (a scholarship still gets you in). New Year's money comes from the staff and sponsors, and is smaller.
  - **Life:** stress settles 5 higher and motivation 2 lower. The home decides where you live, so you can't move house while you're in care. Carers stand in for parents in the words ("your carers", "the home's education budget", 보육원 선생님), the art (a carer in an apron) and the home itself (a long two-storey building with a slide in the yard).
  - **Events:** university volunteers who tutor on Saturdays (math and English up), letters and a little money from a sponsor, and the day classmates find out where you live; the early events (the newborn weeks, the 사주 reading by a volunteer, the 돌잡이, the first word) have their own versions.
  - **Leaving care at 18 (보호종료):** after the first 수능, choose to leave or to stay on in the home until 24. Leaving pays the province's real one-off settlement (자립정착금: ₩20M in Seoul; ₩15M in Gyeonggi, Daejeon and Jeju; ₩12M in Busan; ₩10M elsewhere) into your savings and the real allowance (자립수당, ₩500,000 a month for five years) as your budget, which doesn't halve from the fourth try; living alone costs a block and stress settles 7 higher. Staying keeps a bed, meals and calmer days but no money until you leave, at 24 at the latest. Once you've left you can move like anyone.
  - The Family strip shows "Children's home" and what the home pays (or your allowance after leaving) instead of an income class.
- **Your family's wealth class, always on screen.** A Family strip under the stats shows the household's current income class (low, lower-middle, middle, upper-middle or high income) with a five-step meter for its income fifth. Once family-fortune events have moved it, it says how far from birth (green "↑ Up from middle income at birth", red "↓ Down…"), and the strip flashes when the class changes.
- **A map of Korea.** An interactive map of the peninsula with the real borders of all 17 provinces and all 250 cities, counties and districts (KOSTAT census boundaries; the North faded above a dashed DMZ), every place at its real longitude and latitude, and Seoul's six areas in a magnified inset drawn from its real 25 districts. Your home area is shaded, and your current school is a star where it really stands. Without `places.js` the map falls back to a hand-drawn outline.
  - **Birth:** a pulsing pin shows where your family starts.
  - **Always on screen:** the map sits beside the scene (below it on phones) with your home pinned and the places you could move to right now in blue. Tap any place to open the move dialog on it. After a move, the route stays drawn until your next turn.
  - **Moving house:** the Move house dialog is the map. Dots are red for home, blue where you can move and grey where you can't. Tap (or Tab and Enter) a place to see a card with what moving there would change, the quotas it brings, or why you can't go. A dashed route runs from home to your pick. The same places are still available as a list underneath.
- **Aptitude, always on screen.** Aptitude multiplies every study gain in a subject (and its school lessons), so maxing it out takes 1 / aptitude of the average time. Every ability bar ends in stars (· below 0.88, ★ to 1.05, ★★ to 1.25, ★★★ to 1.45, ★★★★ above, gold from ★★★), and each study card shows the stars and the saving in words, such as "20% less study to max out" or "15% more study to max out". The bar's tooltip gives the exact multiplier. A 돌잡이 subject item adds 0.25, so it often earns an extra star.
- **Luck.** Life is mostly luck, and the game says so:
  - **A fortune-teller's reading (사주) at birth** reveals a hidden fortune, from great to rough. It tilts the odds of every good and bad event (weight × 1 + 0.6 × fortune, for an event tagged good or bad) and adds a little to 수능 day. Lives with a good reading average about 15 points more on their first 수능 than lives with a rough one.
  - **The family's fortunes change** about twice a life, each time moving it a whole income fifth with its education budget. The rates follow real figures (see `data/korea-stats.md`): a promotion or a business taking off (about 47% of lives) or a layoff or a business going under (about 51%); a parent's serious illness (about 17%, from cancer rates in parents' 40s and 50s); an inheritance (about 5%; a lottery jackpot is a one-in-a-hundred twist on that, since the odds are 1 in 8,145,060 a ticket); a jeonse deposit scam (about 1.5%, from 41,559 victims nationally in three years). A family that can no longer pay a private school's fees moves you to the public one.
  - **Your own body:** weeks in hospital (two blocks lost) in about a quarter of lives, and a serious car accident (−35 health, three blocks) in about 4%: about 0.25% of children are hurt in traffic each year.
  - **Lucky breaks:** a teacher who makes a subject click (aptitude +0.2), or a sudden passion for one (aptitude +0.3 and a jump in ability).
  - **Middle 2 rewires everyone:** every aptitude shifts, by a shared amount (sd 0.2: late bloomer or early peaker) plus one per subject (sd 0.18), from its own seeded stream.
  - **수능 day:** the whole day goes well or badly together (a shift of sd 0.35 on every subject), each paper fits you better or worse (sd 0.18 a subject, mocks too), and about half the time something happens: a sniffling neighbour, a lucky guess, a killer question you'd just drilled, a calm clear head, or (rarely) answers marked one row off on the OMR card.
  - **Admissions:** each university's real cut moves a little every year without warning (sd 0.45 percentile points: a 펑크 or a 폭발), and a rejection can still turn into a February waitlist call (추가합격) at a quarter of the chance you had.
  - Everything is seeded: the same seed gives the same fortune, family shocks happen on the same stream, and 수능 day and the paper's fit depend only on the seed and the sitting, never on how you played.
- **Families from real income data:**
  - You're born into one of the five national household-income fifths, each with a 20% chance.
  - The family sets your education budget. Money the parents don't spend on you that turn stays with them.
  - Your own savings (New Year's money from relatives, gifts, a part-time job from High 1) cover anything over the budget.

  | Family | Household income (a month) | Education budget (a month) | Head start at age 6 | Pays for a retake academy |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | Low income (bottom fifth) | about ₩1.25M | ₩205,000 | −0.10 | no (scholarship only) |
  | Lower-middle income | about ₩3.0M | ₩320,000 | −0.05 | no (scholarship only) |
  | Middle income | about ₩4.9M | ₩470,000 | 0 | yes |
  | Upper-middle income | about ₩7.7M | ₩570,000 | +0.05 | yes |
  | High income (top fifth) | about ₩13.8M | ₩676,000 | +0.10 | yes |

  The budgets are the 2024 private-education survey's spending per student: ₩205,000 in households under ₩3M a month, ₩676,000 at ₩8M and above, ₩474,000 on average; the middle fifths are interpolated.

  - **Incomes:** from the 2024 household finance survey (가계금융복지조사). The top fifth averages ₩166M a year and all households ₩71.85M; the middle fifths are interpolated.
  - **Budgets:** follow the 2024 private-education survey (초중고 사교육비조사), which found ₩205,000 a month per student in households under ₩3M and ₩676,000 in households at ₩8M and above. The top fifth's figure is extrapolated above that bracket.
  - **Head start** is in ability units (sd of 수능 takers): books at home and parents' schooling.
  - From the 4th attempt on, parents pay only half.
- **School grades (내신) in high school.** From High 1 to the first semester of High 3 you sit a midterm and a final each semester (High 1–2: one per season turn; High 3: April and July), ten exams in all. Each is graded against the classmates in your year at your school on the 5-grade relative scale used for everyone entering high school from 2025 (MOE, 2023): grade 1 is the top 10%, 2 to 34%, 3 to 66%, 4 to 90%, 5 the rest. The report card after each exam shows each subject's raw score, its absolute level (A–E) and its grade; after a final it adds the semester grades (35% midterm, 40% final, 25% performance assessment, which follows your motivation) and the running average.
  - **Where you study changes your grades.** Your classmates' ability centres on the typical line for your year; at a 자사고 it is 0.7 higher, at a science school 0.9 (1.5 in math and science), at a foreign-language school 0.6 (1.3 in English), at an arts school 0.4 lower and at a vocational school 0.9 lower, and ordinary schools in Gangnam (+0.35) and Seoul's academy districts (+0.25) are a little stronger. So the same ability earns a worse grade at a selective school (the real 내신 불리); 학생부종합 readers know this and read those averages 0.35–0.7 of a grade better, while vocational and arts schools' averages read worse (and count 0.5 / 0.3 of a grade worse in 교과, where most places are built for general schools).
  - **Exam prep (내신 대비)** is a new activity: studying your school's own past papers adds up to +0.22 ability on the day of the next exam, counts as study for stress, and adds only a little to real ability. Every exam is a little stressful (+3), and a better report than last time lifts motivation (a worse one stings).
  - Korean History is graded in High 1 only, and the subjects follow your track after High 1.
- **The school record (학생부).** A 🗂️ School record panel under the ability bars shows the three qualities universities assess in 학생부종합 since 2024 (학업역량 academic, 진로역량 career, 공동체역량 community; five-university joint study, 2022), your 내신 average and semester averages, intended field, club, volunteering hours and research reports. It's built from High 1 to the end of August of High 3, then closed (retakers apply with it as it was).
  - **What builds it:** the career survey in High 1 (medicine, science and engineering, humanities and social sciences, or business and economics; the 진로 event), the club fair (an academic club in your field, a volunteering club, or a band or sports club), and three new activities while it's open: **Club** (community, and career for an academic club), **Volunteering** (community) and **Research project** (career and teachers' comments, with a little study in the field's subjects). Teachers' subject comments (세특) grow every turn with attention in class and the share of your time spent studying; reading still helps there (individual reading lists stopped counting in 2024). A class office and the school festival add community.
  - **Events:** a class presentation (go deep for a strong comment at the cost of a block), and a nudge when your intended field doesn't fit your track (medicine on the humanities track) to change it.
  - **Scores:** academic = 70% your 내신 (100 at 1.0, 35 points less per grade) + 30% comments; career and community are saturating curves of what you've done (community starts at 20). The record score universities use is 65% academic, 20% career, 15% community; medical schools read a non-medical field's career at 60%, science institutes a non-science one at 70%.
- **Early admission (수시).** About 80% of university places are filled in 수시 (80.8% in the 2028 plan; Seoul's top schools keep about 40% for 정시, provincial universities fill about 90% in 수시). In September of every exam year, after the September mock, the 수시 screen lists every university with the types it runs, each with your chance, its minimum 수능 grades (수능 최저) and how likely you are to meet them from your mocks:
  - **학생부교과 (school grades):** your 내신 average against the school's line, often with a 최저; the top schools take only this year's graduates.
  - **학생부종합 (record review):** your record score; at SNU, Yonsei, medical schools, KAIST, POSTECH and a few others a document round in October (about three times as many pass as are admitted), then an interview after the 수능 (prepare with teachers, reread your record, or rest).
  - **논술 (essay exam):** your Korean and your math (sciences) or social studies against a line above the school's 정시 level; never better than 18%, usually with a 최저.
  - **Quotas:** 지역인재 (regional talent) at medical schools and the regional national university, 농어촌 (rural) and 기회균형 (opportunity balance: children in care and about half of the lowest income fifth) make the 교과 and 종합 lines easier.
  - **Up to six applications** (KAIST doesn't count, as in reality). Every 수시 application rolls its own hidden swing in the line, and a rejection can still come good off the waitlist.
  - **수시 납치:** any 수시 offer, even off a waitlist, rules out 정시 that year. You choose among your 수시 offers (or 반수 at one, or retake), so applying to a school below what your 수능 might reach is a real risk. The screen warns in red.
  - **Retakers** apply with the 내신 and record they left school with (교과 at the top schools is closed to them); students who left school (검정고시) can apply only to essay exams. Saves from before v6.0 that were already in high school have no record and play 정시 only.
- **More universities.** The list now has 40 schools: Korea's women's universities (Ewha, Sookmyung, Sungshin, Seoul Women's, Duksung, Dongduk, and Kwangju Women's University among the regional ones), which admit women only (a male player sees them closed in both rounds), the next tier of Seoul schools (Seoul National University of Science and Technology, Kwangwoon, Sejong, Myongji, Dankook), Incheon National University, Chonnam and Chungnam National University (now the local-quota schools for Gwangju, Haenam and Daejeon), and KAIST and POSTECH, which admit only in 수시 (sciences only).

- **Condition:**
  - **Health** below 40 slows study.
  - **Stress** above 75 slows study, and 85+ risks burnout (−2 blocks next turn).
  - **Motivation** multiplies study gains. It drifts back toward a baseline that sags with every extra year.
  - **Being bullied** cuts study efficiency by 10–30% and wears down stress, motivation and health every turn until it's resolved (see above).
  - Stress and health also drift toward a resting level each turn, so a steady workload settles instead of spiralling.
  - High stress or low health on exam day lowers your scores.

Fixed milestones always fire: the free-semester career week (Middle 1), 중2 (Middle 2) and D-100 (August of each 수능 year).
- **Bullying (학교폭력)**, a storyline that lasts until it's resolved:
  - **When:** it can start in Elementary 3 to High 2, most often in elementary school, as in the national survey (4.2% of elementary, 1.6% of middle and 0.5% of high school students a year in 2024): about three in four episodes start in elementary school, one in five in middle school. It's twice as likely for a year after you move (Daechi-dong) or change schools, and 1.4× as likely when your last plan had no time with friends. Each earlier episode halves the chance, and a new episode can't start within a year of the last one ending. About 3 in 10 lives meet it.
  - **Severity:** mild, serious or severe. It starts mild, or serious (more often in middle school).
  - **What it costs, every turn it lasts:** study efficiency drops 10% per severity level, so −10%, −20% or −30%. If someone is supporting you (parents who know, the Wee class counselor, friends you've made time for), it drops 30% less. It also adds stress (+4 per level), drains motivation (−3 per level) and health (−2 per level, or −1 with support). Severe bullying costs a block a turn (days you can't face school).
  - **The first choice:** tell your homeroom teacher, tell your parents (support, but the call can backfire), fight back, or keep quiet.
  - **Every turn after that:** a follow-up asks what you do now:
    - report it to the school violence committee (학폭위): stressful, but the likeliest to end it;
    - talk to the Wee class counselor: gives support and often eases it;
    - stick close to your friends: works much better if you've been making time for them;
    - fight back: a gamble that often makes it worse;
    - endure it in silence: it usually gets worse.
  - **When it turns severe,** the options are reporting it with your parents (it stops at once 60–70% of the time), changing schools (almost always ends it, at the cost of stress, motivation and a block), the counselor, or enduring.
  - **How it ends:** a choice that works, the March class reshuffle (60%, 40% or 20% by severity), starting a new school (70%), or leaving high school.
  - **On screen:** the Condition panel shows a red line with the severity and its cost while it lasts. Every step is in the life log.
  - **With names:** the bully is one of your bossier or more troubled classmates (or an older kid), and the follow-ups name the friend who might stand by you. Names only: the rates and odds are the same.
  - **Cost by High 3:** in a paired test (the same 200 lives cloned at the start of Middle 1 with serious bullying), reporting it costs about 1 point of average ability entering High 3 and enduring it about 4.

## How the exam is simulated

- **Ability:** each subject has a latent ability θ on the scale of the 수능 test takers (mean 0, sd 1). The bars show it as 0–100, where 50 + 16.7·θ, so 50 is average and 100 is θ = 3. At age 6, θ is your talent minus 2.4, so a child of average talent starts around 10.
- **Questions** follow the real layouts: 국어 and 영어 have 45 questions, 수학 has 30 (some short-answer), 한국사 and each 탐구 have 20, with 2-, 3- and 4-point items.
  - Each question has an item-response curve: P(correct) = c + (1 − c) / (1 + e^(−1.7·a·(θ − b))).
  - Guessing c is 0.2 for multiple choice and 0 for short answer.
  - Each paper places a few "killer" questions among the high-point items. In the 탐구 papers they are hard enough that only about 0.5–2% of a subject's takers score 50/50, so a perfect inquiry paper is worth a 99–100 percentile, as in real years.
- **Year-to-year difficulty:** each paper gets a random shift, which makes 불수능 and 물수능 years. The score report's headline names an unusually hard or easy section.
- **The cohort:** each sitting simulates 3,000 test takers per subject. Your standard score, percentile and grade come from your place among them. Inquiry pools differ in strength (Physics I takers are stronger than Earth Science I takers), so the subject choice matters.
- **Exam day:** your ability on the day is θ plus:
  - a stress penalty (above 65) and a health penalty (below 45), each at most −0.25;
  - first-수능 nerves, which practice reduces;
  - the night-before and lunch-box choices;
  - random day form, whose spread shrinks with exam stamina;
  - **luck:** a whole-day swing on 수능 day (sd 0.35 θ on every subject, plus 0.06 × your fortune), how well each paper fits you (sd 0.18 θ a subject, mocks too), and the day's event if there is one (−0.1 on every subject for a sniffling neighbour, +0.08 for a clear head, +0.3 or +0.4 on one subject for a lucky guess or a drilled killer question, −0.6 on one subject for an OMR slip).
- **Learning:**
  - each block on a subject adds 0.24 × subject rate × aptitude × method speed × condition × headroom(θ) × √(months in the turn), times 0.32 during the school years (Elementary 1 to High 2);
  - **school lessons** add to every subject each turn on their own, whatever you do with your blocks, from Elementary 1 through High 3 (not after leaving school): 0.4 a year × the months in the turn / 12 × subject rate × aptitude × how far behind the class you are × attention × headroom(θ). "Behind" is 0.5 + 0.5 × (the typical level for your grade − your θ), kept between 0.4 and 1, so lessons help most when you're behind and a little even when you're far ahead. Attention is 0.6 + 0.4 × motivation/100, times the health and bullying factors. The typical level climbs 0.28 a school year from −2.4 at Elementary 1 (the balanced bot's path); class ranks and the GED use the same line. For an average child, lessons add up to about a third of the climb to High 3. The diary shows each turn's lesson total;
  - condition = the motivation, health and stress factors, times the bullying factor (1, or 0.9–0.7) while you're being bullied;
  - headroom(θ) = e^(−0.62·max(0, θ + 0.8)) gives diminishing returns: going from average to top 4% is far quicker than from top 4% to a perfect score;
  - a second block on the same subject in one turn counts ×0.77, a third ×0.63, and so on;
  - **no decay:** ability only goes up. A subject you don't study stays where it is;
  - subject rates are 국어 0.8, 수학 1.0, 영어 0.95, 한국사 1.6, 탐구 1.3;
  - **early childhood:** each block of play adds 0.1 × its share of a subject × aptitude × condition × headroom(θ) (picture books: Korean 0.6, history 0.3, social studies 0.45; blocks: math 0.7, science 0.5; songs: English 0.8, Korean 0.15; worksheets: Korean and math 0.5), and kindergarten lessons add 0.12 a year × subject rate × aptitude × the kindergarten's multiplier × condition × headroom(θ).

### Early admission: 수시 (the model)

- **School grades (내신):** in each graded subject your ability on the day is θ + prep (up to +0.22) − a stress hit above 70 + noise (sd 0.22). Your classmates' ability is normal around the typical line for your grade + 0.25 + your school's shift (see School grades above), sd 0.85 (0.5–0.9 at special schools). The share of classmates above you sets the grade by the 10/34/66/90% cuts; the raw score is 100 / (1 + e^(−1.3·(θ − classmates' mean + 0.5))). The semester grade combines 35% midterm, 40% final and 25% performance assessment; the 내신 average is every semester grade of every subject, equally weighted (real schools weight by credit hours). Every exam uses its own seeded stream, so it never moves the main RNG.
- **The record score:** 65% academic + 20% career + 15% community. Academic = 0.7 × (100 − 35 × (내신 average − 1 − the school's reading)) + 0.3 × comments, where comments = 100 × (1 − e^(−points/18)) and points come from attention × (0.35 + 0.65 × your study share) each month, research projects and reading; career = 100 × (1 − e^(−points/14)) from research projects and an academic club (× 1 if your field fits your track, 0.4 if not, 0.9 before the track choice) plus 4 for having a field; community = 20 + 80 × (1 − e^(−points/12)) from clubs, volunteering, a class office, the festival and time with friends.
- **교과:** chance = logistic((line − your average) / (0.05 + 0.05 × (line − 1))), so a tenth of a grade matters most at the top. Vocational and arts-school averages count 0.5 and 0.3 of a grade worse.
- **종합:** chance = logistic((record score − line) / 5). With an interview, the document round passes you at logistic((score − line + 7) / 5) (October), and the final odds, given that, use your score plus the interview prep (+3 mock interviews, +1.5 rereading, 0 resting) and a day's swing (sd 3).
- **논술:** chance = 0.18 × logistic((0.6 × Korean + 0.4 × (Math on sciences, social studies on humanities) − (the θ at the school's 정시 percentile + 0.6)) / 0.3).
- **Quotas:** 지역인재 makes the 교과 line 0.25 easier and the 종합 line 6 points easier; 농어촌 and 기회균형 0.3 and 8.
- **On the screen** each option shows its chance after the 최저: the record/grades/essay part times the chance of meeting the 최저, estimated from this year's latest mock (each grade ± about one on the day; 400 draws from a fixed stream). Labels as in 정시.
- **Results:** each application rolls its own stream; the line moves by a hidden sd of 0.06 grades (교과) or 2 points (종합); a missed 최저 is a rejection; a rejection can still come good off the waitlist at 25% of the real chance. Any 수시 offer skips the 정시 screen.

## After the exam: the next ten years

The epilogue's logic is in `engine.js` (`startEpilogue`, `epiChoose`), its figures in `data/korea-stats.md` sections 13–15.

- **University tier** comes from where you enrolled: medical school; SKY; top Seoul (Sungkyunkwan to the University of Seoul); mid (Konkuk to Ajou, with Pusan and Kyungpook National); other four-year universities; junior college; none.
- **GPA** = 3.35 + the university plan (library +0.35, balance +0.1, campus life −0.25) + 0.25 × (ability − 1) + noise (sd 0.3), on 4.5; the returning-student effect adds 0.1 after serving at the end of year 1.
- **Experience** adds up from the plan (balance 0.5, exchange 1, a year off 0.8, campus life 0.3) and service (air force 0.3, social service 0.2, navy and marines 0.1).
- **Each year of trying** for a target succeeds with chance sigmoid(tier row + field + 0.8 × (GPA − 3.35) + 0.35 × experience + ability + 0.25 × fortune + 0.35 a year of retrying), with weights by target: the school counts most for big firms and law school, not at all for blind-hired public enterprises and the civil service exam, where the ability you finished school with counts instead. The field term is the logit gap between your major's real employment rate and the 69.5% average. Three failed years and you take what comes (a small firm, a service job, part-time work or still looking).
- **Pay at 30** is the job's level (big firm ₩5.2M a month, from the ₩50M graduate starting pay plus raises; SME ₩3.0M; resident ₩6.07M; others estimated), ±2% a year in the job beyond four, +2.5% per birth fifth above the middle (half the real top-vs-bottom gap, since the rest comes through school) and noise (sd 12%).
- **Your fifth at 30** ranks your pay among all Korean wage earners (cuts ₩2.0M, ₩2.55M, ₩3.35M, ₩4.9M a month, from the official pay bands); no earnings is the bottom fifth. Your family's fifth is its household income at your birth. So the comparison is rank to rank, as mobility studies do it, but between a worker and a household.

What the tables show:

- **School lifts everyone, habits decide the rest:** lessons carry even the slacker to 56 by High 3, and the same random talents end up anywhere from 56 to 69 on average depending on how you spend your free time, from birth on.
- **Traits are a head start, not a destiny:** a 돌잡이 subject (pencil, microphone, stethoscope) is worth a few points to a bot that spreads its study, and more to a player who leans into that subject; the temperaments mostly make life more comfortable.
- **Luck now decides a lot:** two lives with the same habits and similar ability can end 60 points apart. For the balanced bot, ability entering High 3 explains only about a third of its first total. The smart bot is squeezed by the top of the scale, so its spread widens less, but strong play still wins on average (about 20 points over the balanced bot).
- **Family income matters, but it moves:** families change fifths two or three times a life, so where you're born matters less than it did, and a promotion or a layoff in High school can matter more. Retakes close much of the gap either way.
- **Where you live matters too:** Gangnam's academies give the strongest start, while local and rural quotas help students outside the capital (the region groups above are small, so treat them as a direction, not exact rates).
- **School choice nudges rather than decides:** the bot that tries for selective schools mostly lands in private ones by lottery (a few get into 자사고 or an international middle school) and does slightly better; the smart bot rarely studies far enough ahead in math and science for a science school; a vocational school costs a 수능 candidate about 12 points of total and a block a turn, though its easier 내신 opens 수시 doors (see below).
- **A children's home is the hardest start, not a closed door:** the same lives start 3 points lower and score 9 lower on a first try, and 73% reach an in-Seoul+ university against 81% with both parents (기회균형 helps in 수시); the leaving-care money then makes self-study retakes affordable.
- **Leaving school is a trade, not a shortcut:** the extra hours almost make up for the lost lessons, motivation and the GED, but not quite, and you lose the quotas; what you gain is freedom from school (and bullying) and a spare year if you sit early.
- **수시 adds chances, but they cost something:** with the same lives, the balanced bot reaches in-Seoul+ 49% of the time against 39% with 정시 only, mostly through essay exams and record reviews at schools above its 정시 level. The smart bot gains little (83% against 81%) and loses a few SKY+ places (8% against 10%): an early offer from a school the September mock made look ambitious can turn out below what the 수능 would have reached, and it rules out 정시 (수시 납치).
- **The record is what 수시 rewards:** the bot that spends one block a turn on exam prep, research projects, its club and volunteering scores 6 points less on the 수능 but doubles its SKY+ share (16%) and gets an early offer 54% of the time, through record reviews. Bots that build no record sit at a record score of 40–50, below the lines of most Seoul schools' record reviews, so for them 수시 is mostly a lottery of essay exams.
- **Plausibility:** in reality about 80% of places go to 수시 and the average applicant sends 4.96 applications; the bots send 2–5 and only above their 정시 level, so their 20–55% "enrolled via 수시" is lower than the national share, which is dominated by students applying to schools at or below their level and by provincial universities that fill about 90% in 수시. A vocational school's weaker classmates give an easy 내신 (2.0), which counts 0.5 of a grade worse in 교과 but still opens record reviews and the 기회균형 and 농어촌 quotas, so 31% of those lives reach in-Seoul+ against 18% with 정시 only (the same lives, measured separately).
- **Women's universities** open about one more option in the 85–93 band to girls; with the same lives, 5% of girls enroll at one, and the overall numbers barely move.
- **Retakes** add about 10–20 points of 국수탐 standard total each.
- **Medical school** takes talent, a sciences track and usually several years: the bot chasing it rarely gets in on the first try, but a near-optimal player who keeps retaking usually makes it within five.

## Files

- `places.js`: generated by `tools/build_places.py` (don't edit by hand): province and municipality outlines, each game place's real districts and schools, and the special schools nationwide. The engine reads it for real school names and runs the same without it.
- `engine.js`: the game, with no DOM, so it runs in Node. It holds no display text: log entries, events and outcomes are message keys with values.
  - `Game` holds the state and the screen queue: `advance(plan, method)`, `choose(i)`, `dismiss()`, `chooseTrack(track, inq)`, `apply(ids)`, `decide(d)`, and after the ending `startEpilogue()` / `epiChoose(id)`.
  - `achievements(meta)` lists the achievements a life has earned; `birthInfo()` rebuilds where and into what you were born; `Game.dailySeed(date)` is today's life. Other systems can add lines to the epilogue's "where they are now" through `EPILOGUE_HOOKS`.
  - It also holds the life stages, families, the exam simulation (`gradeSubject`), events, universities and constants.
- `strings.js`: every piece of text, as an English and a Korean dictionary with the same keys. `make(lang, ctx)` returns the translator, including Korean money (만/억) and particle (이/가) handling.
- `engine.js` people (v6.1): `g.social` holds the people (`soc()` builds it for old saves), `socialTurn(plan)` runs a turn of friendships, `rollSocial` the social events, `roleOf(p)` and `setFocus(id)` serve the Friends panel, and `friendOutcomes(n)` returns where your closest friends ended up: up to n (default 5) of `{ id, s, n, x, lk, role, cl, from, sci, total, pct, path: "uni" | "retake" | "work", uni, aim }`, closest first.
- `art.js`: the artwork, as SVG strings built in code (no DOM, no image files; the only words drawn are names app.js passes in, in the current language). The places are sets (`SETS`) built from a few pieces: `room()` (a room in one-point perspective), `proj()` (a camera for spaces measured in metres, like the corridor), `boxP()` (furniture and buildings in perspective), `windowView()` / `landscape()` (the view by region and season), `sky()` by time of day, and light (`pool`, `shadow`, `ao`, `vignette`); gradients a drawing uses are written once into its own `<defs>`. `scene(ctx)` draws the life-stage scene with the avatar, `eventArt(ctx)` an event's illustration (data-driven through `EVENT_ART`, with fallbacks for unknown event ids), `screenArt(name, ctx)` the birth, track, mock, exam, report, application, offers and ending pictures, `cutscene(name, ctx)` a cutscene's shots (each an SVG plus its length; the motion is CSS keyframes in `styles.css`), and `moodOf(game)` / `lookFor(seed, sex)` the avatar's mood and looks, `figureFor(stage)` the body and clothes for a life stage, `avatar(o)` a person (feet at 0, 0; ages, outfits, job gear, poses and props are listed in the comment above it), and `portrait` / `portraitFor(stage, look, mood)` a head-and-shoulders portrait for the Friends panel. An event that names people ({ lk, x } in `ctx.people`) draws them beside you. `ctx.tone` (v6.5, from the engine's `tone` on each outcome, `promptTone`, `screenTone` and `recentTone`) sets a drawing's mood through `withMood`: its sky, lamps, the `grade()` veils, the sets for the worst moments and faces that never smile in a bad moment. Colours are `--a-*` tokens in `styles.css`.
- `app.js`: UI only. It renders the state and whatever screen is first in `game.queue`, and plays a milestone's cutscene first (`pendingCut` / `playCut`).
